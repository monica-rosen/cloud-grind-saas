# Cloud Grind — Customer Personas

**Use these personas to write copy that speaks directly to your customer's life, not to coffee culture.**

---

## Persona 1: Alex — The Creative Entrepreneur

### Demographics
- **Age:** 28–38
- **Gender:** Any (50/50 M/F)
- **Income:** $60K–$120K
- **Location:** Urban (NYC, LA, Austin, SF, Brooklyn, Seattle, Portland)
- **Occupation:** Designer, writer, artist, founder, creative director, illustrator, photographer, UX designer

### Psychographics
- **Identity:** Maker. Builds things. Values craft and authenticity.
- **Values:** Uniqueness, creative autonomy, supporting independent makers, aesthetic quality, authenticity over scale
- **Status anxiety:** Wants to be "in the know" but rejects gatekeeping. Hates corporate culture. Supports indie brands.
- **Media:** Instagram, Substack, Dribbble, Twitter, TikTok. Reads design blogs, creative newsletters, indie publications.
- **Spending:** Will pay premium for quality + story. Invests in tools that support creative work (good laptop, ergonomic chair, specialty coffee).
- **Social:** Shares discoveries with peers. Influences recommendations in their circle.

### Coffee Behavior
- **Frequency:** 4–6 bags/month
- **Price sensitivity:** Will pay $22–28/bag if quality + story align
- **Purchase driver:** Sourcing story, origin transparency, aesthetic packaging, creator credibility
- **Ritual:** Coffee is part of creative ritual. Brewing is meditation. Shares coffee pics on Instagram.
- **Pain point:** Tired of coffee gatekeeping (specialty roasters who act superior). Wants quality without snobbery.

### Lifestyle Context
- **Morning:** Works early. Coffee fuels 6am–9am deep work window.
- **Workspace:** Home studio, coworking space, or hybrid. Values control over environment.
- **Stress:** Client deadlines, creative block, imposter syndrome, always learning.
- **Motivation:** Love the work. Financial security is nice but secondary to autonomy + meaning.
- **Social:** Follows 200+ creators on Instagram. Cares what peers buy and use.

### How Alex Finds Coffee
- **Influencer mention** (designer they follow mentions it casually)
- **Peer recommendation** (friend from creative community)
- **Instagram explore feed** (aesthetic product photo, relatable copy)
- **Design/creator newsletter** (partner mentions)
- **Substack or blog post** (long-form content about sourcing/roasting)

### Coffee Messaging That Works
✅ "Made by someone who got tired of gatekeeping"  
✅ "Direct trade at prices that don't require a second mortgage"  
✅ "Coffee that's as thoughtful as your creative work"  
✅ "Skip the pretension. Keep the quality."  
✅ "Roasted the way [founder] actually wants to drink it"  

❌ "Award-winning" (unsubstantiated)  
❌ "Treat yourself" (condescending)  
❌ "Artisanal craftsmanship" (gatekeeping language)  
❌ "Limited edition exclusive blend" (fake scarcity)  
❌ "Discover your coffee soulmate" (corny)  

### Alex's Objection & How to Handle
- **"Why $24? Starbucks is $15."** → "Starbucks buys commodity beans. We pay farmers 3x commodity prices. You taste the difference in the cup."
- **"I'm already a coffee snob. Why switch?"** → "This isn't about snobbery—it's about honesty. We'll tell you exactly what you're getting and why it matters."
- **"How do I know it's actually good?"** → "Try a bag. We have a 60-day happy return policy. If it's not your thing, send it back."

### Alex as a Subscriber
- **Conversion path:** Single bag → Trial bundle → Subscription (every 2 weeks, 2 bags/month)
- **Retention lever:** Surprise + delight (occasional limited edition at no extra cost, email with roaster's notes, behind-the-scenes photos)
- **Churn risk:** If copy becomes corporate-sounding or gatekeeping creeps in
- **Lifetime value:** $490/year (2 bags/month, $20.40/bag)
- **Referral rate:** High. Creatives love telling peers about discoveries.

### Writing Example for Alex
**Email subject:** "Kenya arrived. Here's why it tastes different than last month's."  
**Email body:**
> "Hey Alex,
> 
> Your Kenya shipped yesterday. This month's lot is from a farm we've worked with for two years. The farmer, Samuel, increases shade-grown acreage every harvest. It costs more. The coffee tastes cleaner because of it.
> 
> Brew it as a pour-over or espresso. It's bright either way. We'd probably drink it black, but do what works.
> 
> —[Founder]"

## Persona 2: Jordan — The Busy Professional

### Demographics
- **Age:** 32–48
- **Gender:** Any (60% M, 40% F, reflective of corporate demographics)
- **Income:** $80K–$250K+
- **Location:** Suburban + urban (distributed, but some concentration in tech hubs, financial centers)
- **Occupation:** Manager, senior engineer, consultant, product lead, operations director, remote founder, executive

### Psychographics
- **Identity:** Builder. Solves problems. Values efficiency and reliability.
- **Values:** Time, reliability, quality outcomes, simplicity, getting things done
- **Status anxiety:** Busy = important. But also exhausted. Wants to feel less frantic.
- **Media:** Email newsletters, podcasts (while commuting/running), LinkedIn, Twitter. Reads business/tech news, not lifestyle content.
- **Spending:** Will pay for convenience and quality. Not wasteful but values ROI.
- **Social:** Less likely to share on Instagram. Shares recommendations in private Slack channels or one-on-one conversations.

### Coffee Behavior
- **Frequency:** 4–5 bags/month (sometimes 6, depending on work intensity)
- **Price sensitivity:** Will pay $18–24/bag if it's convenient + consistent
- **Purchase driver:** Consistency, reliability, subscription ease, works-with-busy-schedule messaging
- **Ritual:** Coffee is functional. Tastes good is important. Ritual is secondary to results.
- **Pain point:** Too many decisions. Wants one good coffee source that "just works."

### Lifestyle Context
- **Morning:** Rushed. Coffee in hand while answering emails. 6:30am–7:30am window.
- **Workspace:** Office + home + meetings. Often in back-to-back calls. Coffee at desk.
- **Stress:** Deadlines, people management, context switching, always behind, trying to exercise, remembering to drink water.
- **Motivation:** Security, impact, autonomy. Works hard to have options. Family/kids increasingly important.
- **Social:** Follows 50–100 people on LinkedIn. Rarely comments. Consumes content fast.

### How Jordan Finds Coffee
- **Podcast ad** or sponsor mention (Lenny's Podcast, Masters of Scale, business podcast)
- **Recommendation from colleague/manager** ("This is what I order")
- **Google search** "best coffee subscription" or "specialty coffee direct"
- **Work Slack** (someone in #random mentions it)
- **Newsletter ad** (product/growth/tech newsletter Jordan reads)

### Coffee Messaging That Works
✅ "Set it and forget it. One less decision to make."  
✅ "Ships in 2 days. Always fresh."  
✅ "Same coffee every month. No surprises. No guessing."  
✅ "Subscription saves you 15%. Pause anytime."  
✅ "Built by someone who was tired of bad coffee at the office."  

❌ "Discover yourself through coffee" (too lifestyle-y)  
❌ "Premium artisanal experience" (too flowery)  
❌ "Join our coffee community" (Jordan doesn't have time for community)  
❌ "Treat yourself" (patronizing)  
❌ "Award-winning origins" (Jordan doesn't care, just wants good)  

### Jordan's Objection & How to Handle
- **"Why not just buy local?"** → "You could. Most local roasters don't ship. Ours arrives 2 days after roasting. Fresh + convenient."
- **"I don't have time to think about coffee."** → "Exactly. Subscribe once. Same coffee monthly. $20.40/bag. Pause whenever."
- **"How do I know it's good?"** → "Single origin means you'll taste it more clearly. We include brewing notes. Try it for a month. If it's not your thing, no problem."

### Jordan as a Subscriber
- **Conversion path:** Website discovery → Subscription (every 4 weeks, 1 bag/month) → Occasional gift bags
- **Retention lever:** Consistency (same coffee arriving on time), convenience (one click to pause), occasional premium emails with brewing tips
- **Churn risk:** If subscription is complex to manage or coffee quality becomes inconsistent
- **Lifetime value:** $245/year (1 bag/month, $20.40/bag) + occasional gift purchases
- **Referral rate:** Moderate. Will recommend if asked but doesn't proactively share.

### Writing Example for Jordan
**Email subject:** "Your subscription shipped. Here's everything you need to know."  
**Email body:**
> "Jordan,
> 
> Your order shipped today. It'll arrive Thursday.
> 
> **This month:** Colombia. Balanced. Works great for espresso or drip.
> 
> **Brewing tip:** Slightly cooler water (195°F) brings out sweetness. Hotter (205°F) brings out structure.
> 
> **Next shipment:** Scheduled for 28 days from today. Pause or skip anytime via your account.
> 
> Questions? Reply to this email. We read everything.
> 
> —[Founder]"

## Persona 3: Casey — The Social-First Millennial

### Demographics
- **Age:** 24–35
- **Gender:** Any (equal distribution)
- **Income:** $40K–$90K
- **Location:** Urban (major cities, strong social media presence required)
- **Occupation:** Content creator, marketer, social media manager, junior designer, startup employee, grad student

### Psychographics
- **Identity:** Curator. Shares discoveries. Aesthetic matters.
- **Values:** Authenticity, community, aesthetic, social proof, exclusivity without gatekeeping
- **Status anxiety:** Wants to be "in the know" before it blows up. FOMO is real. Needs social validation.
- **Media:** Instagram, TikTok, BeReal, Discord, Twitter. Watches YouTube creators. Reads nothing long-form.
- **Spending:** Will pay for cool things but price-sensitive. Wants to feel smart about purchases.
- **Social:** Shares constantly. Influences peers through casual Instagram stories and recommendations.

### Coffee Behavior
- **Frequency:** 2–4 bags/month (sometimes buys multiple at once for gifting)
- **Price sensitivity:** Will pay $20–26/bag if it photographs well + has story
- **Purchase driver:** Aesthetic packaging, founder credibility, Instagram/TikTok presence, peer recommendations, limited editions
- **Ritual:** Coffee is part of morning aesthetic. Flat lay worthy. Caption-worthy.
- **Pain point:** Too much choice. Wants to be confident they're choosing "cool."

### Lifestyle Context
- **Morning:** Instagram story of coffee before 8am. Golden hour window is critical.
- **Workspace:** Coffee shop or home with good light. Desk setup matters aesthetically.
- **Stress:** Keeping up, relevance, comparison, FOMO, managing personal brand
- **Motivation:** Creative expression, community, being "in the know," authentic self-presentation
- **Social:** Follows 500+ accounts. Creates content weekly. Cares deeply about follower count and engagement.

### How Casey Finds Coffee
- **Influencer mention** (TikTok coffee creator, design creator using it)
- **Instagram explore page** (scrolling, aesthetic photo catches eye)
- **Friend recommendation** (peer with social media presence tried it)
- **Coffee TikTok** (viral trend or creator recommendation)
- **Gifted by brand** (DM from brand asking for shout-out)

### Coffee Messaging That Works
✅ "Roasted by someone who actually cares, not a corporation"  
✅ "Limited edition drops. When it's gone, it's gone."  
✅ "Tag us in your morning brew. We repost the good ones."  
✅ "Coffee that looks as good as it tastes"  
✅ "Founder-roasted. No gatekeeping. No BS."  

❌ "Luxury experience" (corporate)  
❌ "Join our exclusive community" (too formal)  
❌ "Award-winning" (unverified, dismissive)  
❌ "Support small business" (guilt-based)  
❌ "Indulge yourself" (patronizing)  

### Casey's Objection & How to Handle
- **"Why buy specialty when Starbucks is cheaper?"** → "Starbucks tastes the same every time. Ours tastes like coffee. Plus it photographs better."
- **"How do I know other people like it?"** → "Check our Instagram. Hundreds of people tag us. Real customers, not fake reviews."
- **"Is this just marketing hype?"** → "Nope. The founder left a corporate job to roast. No investors, no corporate parent. It's real."

### Casey as a Subscriber
- **Conversion path:** Single limited edition bag → Trial → Sporadic purchases + gifting → Subscription (if at all)
- **Retention lever:** Limited editions, Instagram shout-outs, founder authenticity, community engagement, surprise gifts
- **Churn risk:** If brand becomes "mainstream" or loses founder credibility
- **Lifetime value:** $200–400/year (lower LTV, but higher referral impact)
- **Referral rate:** Very high. Will recommend and tag on social.

### Writing Example for Casey
**Instagram caption:**
> "New limited edition just dropped: Kenyan micro-lot. 150 bags. Bright, clean, tastes like you're supposed to enjoy it. Link in bio.
> 
> Tag us in your morning brew. We'll repost the good ones. 📸☕"

**Email subject:** "Only 47 bags left of this limited edition"  
**Email body:**
> "Hey Casey,
> 
> Remember that Kenya we roasted last week? 150 bags. 47 left.
> 
> We're not hyping scarcity for marketing. There's literally just not much of this micro-lot. When it's gone, we're moving to the next origin.
> 
> If you want it, grab it. No pressure.
> 
> —[Founder]"

## Cross-Persona Insights

### Universal Truths (Write for All Three)
1. **Honesty > hype.** All three value transparency and authenticity.
2. **Show don't tell.** Describe what coffee does, not what it is.
3. **Respect their time.** Short emails, scannable copy, fast shipping.
4. **No gatekeeping.** None of these personas want to be made to feel stupid about coffee.
5. **Founder credibility matters.** All three care that a real person roasts this, not a corporation.

### Where They Differ (Tailor messaging)
- **Alex:** Lead with story and sourcing. Instagram/Substack friendly.
- **Jordan:** Lead with convenience and consistency. Email-focused, actionable.
- **Casey:** Lead with aesthetic and social proof. Visual-first, limited editions, tag/share mechanics.

## Persona Development Questions (For Your Copy)

When writing any piece of copy, ask:
1. **Who am I writing for?** (Alex / Jordan / Casey / all three?)
2. **What does this person need to hear?** (Story / Reliability / Social proof)
3. **What language do they use?** (Creative speak / Professional brevity / Gen-Z casual)
4. **What will make them convert?** (Authenticity / Convenience / FOMO)
5. **What will make them stay?** (Story + surprises / Consistency / Exclusivity)

## Using Personas in Copywriting

### Email to Alex
- Lead with origin story or sourcing update
- Use first-person (founder voice)
- Assume design/creative background
- Reference community or peer recommendations
- Include behind-the-scenes or visual elements

### Email to Jordan
- Lead with practical info (shipping date, brewing tips)
- Use direct, active voice
- Assume busy schedule
- Focus on convenience and consistency
- Keep it scannable with bullets or short paragraphs

### Instagram Caption for Casey
- Lead with visuals (limited edition, behind-the-scenes)
- Use casual, authentic tone
- Include call-to-action (tag us, share your brew)
- Reference exclusivity or social proof
- Hashtag strategically for discovery

## TL;DR: Three Personas, One Brand

| Persona | Motivation | Messaging | Retention |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Alex (Creative)** | Authenticity + story | "Founder-roasted. Direct trade. No gatekeeping." | Surprise limited editions + behind-the-scenes |
| **Jordan (Professional)** | Convenience + reliability | "Set it and forget it. Same coffee every month." | Consistency + ease of pause/resume |
| **Casey (Social)** | Aesthetic + social proof | "Limited edition. Tag us in your brew." | Exclusivity + community engagement + Instagram shout-outs |

**Write different copy for each. But never sacrifice brand voice. All three should feel like they're buying from the same founder—approachable, minimalist, confident.**
